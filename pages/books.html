Books

The books I've highlighted the most, and a few of the lines I kept.

Principles

Ray Dalio
BusinessThinking

Ray Dalio lays out the rules he developed for decision-making in life and at Bridgewater, the investment firm he founded. The core ideas are radical open-mindedness, radical transparency, and learning from mistakes.

Maturity is the ability to reject good alternatives in order to pursue even better ones.
Pain + Reflection = Progress.

Meditations

Marcus Aurelius
Stoicism

The private notebook of a Roman emperor, written as reminders to himself. He returns again and again to controlling his own judgments, accepting what he can't change, and treating other people well.

The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.
It never ceases to amaze me: we all love ourselves more than other people, but care more about their opinion than our own.

The Laws of Human Nature

Robert Greene
Mind

A guide to the patterns that drive human behavior, including narcissism, envy, conformity, and short-term thinking. Greene uses historical examples to show how to recognize these traits in others and in yourself.

Human desire is never an individual phenomenon. We are social creatures and what we want almost always reflects what other people want.
You need some detachment and perspective. Get your ego to calm down. Remind yourself that winning an argument or proving your point really gets you nowhere in the long run.

Letters From a Stoic

Seneca
Stoicism

A collection of letters Seneca wrote to his friend Lucilius late in life. They give practical advice on fear, wealth, friendship, time, and death from a Stoic point of view.

We suffer more often in imagination than in reality.
It is equally faulty to trust everyone and to trust no one.

101 Essays That Will Change the Way You Think

Brianna Wiest
Living

A collection of short essays on emotions, habits, happiness, and self-sabotage. Most argue that how you interpret your life matters more than the circumstances themselves.

Clarity comes from doing, not thinking about doing.
Most people don’t change until not changing is the less comfortable option.

The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

Eric Jorgenson
BusinessLiving

A compilation of Naval Ravikant's tweets, interviews, and essays, organized into two parts: building wealth and finding happiness. It covers leverage, specific knowledge, long-term thinking, and peace of mind.

Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want. I don’t think most of us realize that’s what it is.
Play iterated games. All the returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest.

The Courage to Be Disliked

Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga
MindLiving

A dialogue between a philosopher and a young man that explains Alfred Adler's psychology. It argues that the past doesn't determine who you are, that most problems are about relationships, and that freedom means not living for others' approval.

We do not lack ability. We just lack courage. It all comes down to courage.
A healthy feeling of inferiority is not something that comes from comparing oneself to others; it comes from one’s comparison with one’s ideal self.

Stillness Is the Key

Ryan Holiday
StoicismLiving

Holiday draws on Stoic, Buddhist, and other traditions to argue that calm focus is the basis for good thinking and good work. The book is split into three parts: mind, spirit, and body.

The one thing you can’t escape in your life is yourself.
This is what the best journals look like. They aren’t for the reader. They are for the writer. To slow the mind down. To wage peace with oneself.

Mastery

Robert Greene
Craft

Greene studies the lives of masters such as Darwin, Faraday, and da Vinci to describe the path to excellence in a field. That path runs from finding your calling, through a long apprenticeship, to creative work.

Some people never become who they are; they stop trusting themselves; they conform to the tastes of others, and they end up wearing a mask that hides their true identity.
The moment that you rest, thinking that you have attained the level you desire, a part of your mind enters a phase of decay.

Clear Thinking

Shane Parrish
Thinking

Parrish argues that most bad decisions happen in ordinary moments, when emotion, ego, social pressure, or habit react before reasoning starts. The book covers how to spot those defaults and set up habits and conditions for better judgment.

Wisdom is turning your future hindsight into your current foresight.
Many people think they’re bad problem solvers when in fact they’re bad problem anticipators.

The 21 Irrefutable Laws of Leadership

John C. Maxwell
Leadership

Maxwell sets out 21 principles of leadership, such as influence, trust, priorities and succession. Each one comes with examples from business, sports, the military and history.

leadership cannot be awarded, appointed, or assigned. It comes only from influence, and that cannot be mandated. It must be earned. The only thing a title can buy is a little time—either to increase your level of influence with others or to undermine it.
Character makes trust possible. And trust makes leadership possible.

Ego Is the Enemy

Ryan Holiday
Stoicism

Holiday argues that ego, meaning an unhealthy belief in your own importance, gets in the way at every stage: when you are starting out, when you succeed and when you fail. He draws on Stoic philosophy and historical figures to make the case for humility and steady work.

What is rare is not raw talent, skill, or even confidence, but humility, diligence, and self-awareness.
Ego needs honors in order to be validated. Confidence, on the other hand, is able to wait and focus on the task at hand regardless of external recognition.

Lessons From an American Stoic

Mark Matousek
Philosophy

Matousek presents Ralph Waldo Emerson's ideas as practical guidance. He organizes them into lessons on originality, perspective, nonconformity, resilience, courage and love, and ties them to Stoic thought.

Seeing your narrative come apart before your eyes proves how arbitrary your personal fictions are.
We underestimate the impact of confidence on our ability to flourish.

The Speed of Trust

Stephen M.R. Covey
Leadership

Covey argues that trust is a measurable factor in business: when trust is high, work moves faster and costs less. He breaks credibility into integrity, intent, capabilities and results, and lists behaviors that build or restore trust.

when trust is low, speed goes down and cost goes up. When trust is high, speed goes up and cost goes down.
Ultimately micromanagers who trust only themselves can take their companies only as far as they themselves can take them.

Aristotle's Way

Edith Hall
Philosophy

Hall, a classicist, explains Aristotle's ethics as a practical guide to a happy life. The book covers finding your potential, making decisions, communicating, building character, friendship and facing death.

Aristotle believed that if you train yourself to be good, by working on your virtues and controlling your vices, you will discover that a happy state of mind comes from habitually doing the right thing.
The trick is to find out what you like doing and have an aptitude for, and then stick at it.

The 9 Types of Leadership

Beatrice Chestnut
LeadershipMind

Chestnut uses the Enneagram to describe nine personality types and how each one tends to lead. She argues that good leadership depends on noticing your own habitual, defensive patterns and working past them.

Most of all, leaders (and all of us) need effective ways to learn to be kinder and more compassionate with ourselves and with others, and less defensive, reactive, and inflexible in the ways we respond to the people and events around us.
the ability to achieve and maintain harmonious and productive working relationships depends on one thing: how well the people involved have the humility and the will to understand and manage themselves and understand and relate well to others.

The 33 Strategies of War

Robert Greene
Thinking

Greene takes 33 strategies from military history and applies them to conflict in everyday life and work. They cover defense, offense, unconventional tactics and self-command.

Getting along with other people is an important skill to have, but it comes with a danger: by always seeking the path of least resistance, the path of conciliation, you forget who you are, and you sink into the center with everyone else.
Unintimidate yourself. Intimidation will always threaten your presence of mind.

The Courage to Be Happy

Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga
MindLiving

This sequel to The Courage to Be Disliked is a dialogue between a philosopher and a young teacher about Alfred Adler's psychology. It covers praise and punishment, self-reliance, work, friendship and love.

The past does not decide now. It is your now that decides the past.
Instead of seeking approval, one has to approve oneself, with one’s own mind.

Heroic Leadership

Chris Lowney
Leadership

Lowney, a former Jesuit seminarian who became a banker, looks at how the Jesuits built a lasting global organization. He identifies four principles behind it: self-awareness, ingenuity, love and heroism.

Only those who know their weaknesses can deal with them or even hope to conquer them.
Heroic leaders don’t bide their time until the big moment comes along; they grasp the opportunities within reach and extract as much richness from them as possible.

The Effective Executive

Peter F. Drucker
Leadership

Drucker describes the habits that make knowledge workers and managers effective: knowing where their time goes, focusing on contribution, building on strengths, doing first things first and making sound decisions.

People in general, and knowledge workers in particular, grow according to the demands they make on themselves.
Strong people always have strong weaknesses too.

Scaling People

Claire Hughes Johnson
Leadership

A manager's handbook from Stripe's former COO on building a company's operating system as it grows: planning, hiring, team development, feedback, and communication. It pairs frameworks with templates and exercises for leaders at growing companies.

I often think of my work as a manager as clearing the path my team will need to travel. Anything that slows them down is on me.
Good communication is about providing timely and honest information, including being willing to acknowledge mistakes. People forgive mistakes, but they lose trust when information is hidden, false, or misleading, or when leadership says something but doesn’t follow through.

Working Backwards

Colin Bryar & Bill Carr
Business

Two longtime Amazon executives describe the company's management practices, including single-threaded leadership, six-page narratives, the PR/FAQ process, Bar Raiser hiring, and input metrics. They show how those practices shaped Kindle, Prime, Prime Video, and AWS.

“The best way to fail at inventing something is by making it somebody’s part-time job.”
There’s a saying often heard at Amazon: “Good intentions don’t work. Mechanisms do.”

Play Bigger

Al Ramadan, Dave Peterson, Christopher Lochhead & Kevin Maney
Business

The authors argue that the most valuable companies create and define new market categories rather than compete in existing ones. The book lays out "category design": framing the problem, writing a point of view, and designing product, company, and category together.

A company that best frames a problem is the company that often comes to define and take the category. Put another way: winning companies today market the problem, not just the solution.
Customers will ask for better. You want to hear different. Look for what’s missing, not what can be improved.

Four Thousand Weeks

Oliver Burkeman
Living

A book about time management that starts from the fact that the average life lasts about four thousand weeks. Burkeman argues that trying to get everything done makes things worse, and that accepting limits and making hard choices leads to a better use of time.

Productivity is a trap. Becoming more efficient just makes you more rushed, and trying to clear the decks simply makes them fill up again faster.
The real measure of any time management technique is whether or not it helps you neglect the right things.

On Writing Well

William Zinsser
Craft

A guide to writing nonfiction, covering clarity, simplicity, cutting clutter, rewriting, and writing in your own voice. Later chapters apply those principles to interviews, travel, memoir, science, business, and criticism.

If you find that writing is hard, it’s because it is hard.
Readers want the person who is talking to them to sound genuine. Therefore a fundamental rule is: be yourself.

The Daily Stoic

Ryan Holiday & Stephen Hanselman
Stoicism

A year of daily readings, each pairing a passage from Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Epictetus, or another Stoic with a short commentary. The readings are grouped into monthly themes such as clarity, judgment, acceptance, and mortality.

Ask yourself: What haven’t I considered? Why is this thing the way it is? Am I part of the problem here or the solution? Could I be wrong here?
“Throw out your conceited opinions, for it is impossible for a person to begin to learn what he thinks he already knows.” —EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.17.1

What You Do Is Who You Are

Ben Horowitz
Leadership

Horowitz argues that a company's culture is defined by what its people actually do, not by stated values. He draws on examples like the Haitian revolution, the samurai, Genghis Khan, and a prison gang leader to show how leaders shape culture through their actions.

If you aren’t yourself, even you won’t follow you.
If somebody behaves in a way you can’t believe, remember that your culture somehow made that acceptable.

Dare to Lead

Brené Brown
Leadership

Brown applies her research on vulnerability, shame, and courage to leadership. She covers how leaders can build trust, give honest feedback, live by their values, and recover from setbacks.

Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind.
Integrity is choosing courage over comfort; it’s choosing what’s right over what’s fun, fast, or easy; and it’s practicing your values, not just professing them.

A Calendar of Wisdom

Leo Tolstoy
Philosophy

A collection Tolstoy compiled late in life with one reading for each day of the year. It mixes his own thoughts with sayings from philosophers, religious texts, and writers on topics like kindness, work, wealth, and self-improvement.

If you want to do a good deed, do it now. The time will pass, and you will not have the chance again.
The more urgently you want to speak, the more likely it is that you will say something foolish.

Fallen Leaves

Will Durant
Philosophy

Short essays the historian wrote near the end of his life, published after his death. He gives his views on youth, aging, religion, war, politics, art, science, education, and death.

Life is that which is discontent, which struggles and seeks, which suffers and creates.
Let us ask the gods not for possessions, but for things to do; happiness is in making things rather than in consuming them.

Lighter

Yung Pueblo
Living

A short book on emotional healing and self-awareness, drawing on meditation practice. It covers letting go of old reactions, managing ego, and how personal change affects relationships and community.

your first reaction is your past your intentional response is your present
Ego craves to win, but loving clarity seeks to understand.

BE 2.0

Jim Collins
Business

An updated edition of Collins's 1992 guide for small and mid-sized companies that want to last. It covers leadership, getting the right people, vision and values, strategy, and day-to-day execution.

How do you know if you have a truly great business? Warren Buffett has the best answer: You don’t have to hold a prayer meeting to raise prices.
Innovation often comes from seeing the relationship between unconnected ideas and melding them together.

What They Forgot to Teach You at School

The School of Life
Living

A collection of short essays on the emotional skills school leaves out. Topics include self-compassion, kindness, repairing relationships, managing moods, and living with anxiety.

Hating ourselves is the easy bit. Learning to give ourselves a break is the true, rare and properly adult achievement.
Small acts of kindness are a way of lending others small change in the currency of hope and courage which we depend on for our emotional survival.

Antifragile

Nassim Nicholas Taleb
Thinking

Taleb argues that some things gain from volatility, stress, and disorder, and calls them antifragile. He applies the idea to health, economics, innovation, and ethics, and argues for limiting downside while leaving room for upside.

Antifragility is beyond resilience or robustness. The resilient resists shocks and stays the same; the antifragile gets better.
Steve Jobs figured out that “you have to work hard to get your thinking clean to make it simple.”

The Bhagavad Gita

Eknath Easwaran (translator)
Philosophy

A Hindu scripture in which Krishna advises the warrior Arjuna before a battle. Easwaran's translation and introductions explain its teachings on duty, selfless action, detachment from results, and meditation.

Since the Self is the core of every personality, no one needs to acquire goodness or compassion; they are already there. All that is necessary is to remove the selfish habits that hide them.
When meditation is mastered, the mind is unwavering like the flame of a lamp in a windless place.

Truman

David McCullough
HistoryLeadership

A biography of Harry S. Truman, from his Missouri farm upbringing through the Senate and his presidency. It covers the end of World War II, the Marshall Plan, and the early Cold War.

Always be nice to the people who can’t talk back to you.
More than once in his presidency, Truman would be remembered saying it was remarkable how much could be accomplished if you didn’t care who received the credit.

Zero to One

Peter Thiel
Business

Thiel's notes on startups, built around the idea that real progress comes from creating new things, not copying existing ones. He argues that great companies escape competition, build monopolies, and rest on a secret others have missed.

What important truth do very few people agree with you on?
The most contrarian thing of all is not to oppose the crowd but to think for yourself.

As a Man Thinketh

James Allen
Living

A short 1903 essay arguing that a person's character and circumstances come from their habitual thoughts. Allen says that changing how you think is the way to change your life.

Men do not attract that which they want, but that which they are.
Men are anxious to improve their circumstances, but are unwilling to improve themselves; they therefore remain bound.

It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work

Jason Fried & David Heinemeier Hansson
Business

The founders of Basecamp describe how they run a calm company. They cover 40-hour weeks, protecting time and attention, writing instead of meeting, and avoiding growth at all costs.

A fractured hour isn’t really an hour—it’s a mess of minutes.
Don’t meet, write. Don’t react, consider.

From Strength to Strength

Arthur C. Brooks
Living

Brooks looks at professional decline in middle age and how to handle it. He suggests shifting from raw problem-solving to wisdom and teaching, letting go of success addiction, and investing in relationships.

Satisfaction comes not from chasing bigger and bigger things, but paying attention to smaller and smaller things.
Success, the fruit of excellence, becomes an addiction. All because of pride.

The Captain Class

Sam Walker
Leadership

A study of the most dominant teams in sports history, which finds that the common factor was not a star player or coach but a captain who did unglamorous work, held the team to a high standard, and led mostly through example and private conversation.

“You do your job so everyone around you can do their job,” Tom Brady once said. “There’s no big secret to it.”
A great leader is dedicated to doing whatever it takes to make success more likely, even if it’s unpopular, or controversial, or outrageous, or completely invisible to others. A leader has to be committed, above all else, to getting it right.

No Rules Rules

Reed Hastings & Erin Meyer
LeadershipBusiness

Netflix's co-founder and a business professor explain the company's culture: hire very strong people, pay them well, ask for constant candid feedback, and then remove most rules and approvals so they can make their own decisions.

If you build an organization made up of high performers, you can eliminate most controls. The denser the talent, the greater the freedom you can offer.
When the boss steps out of the role of “decision approver,” the entire business speeds up and innovation increases.

The CEO Test

Adam Bryant & Kevin Sharer
Leadership

Drawing on interviews with hundreds of CEOs, the authors lay out the core tests of leadership: writing a simple plan, building culture and a strong team, leading change, listening, and handling a crisis.

Simplifying complexity is a leader's superpower.
Though many people are loath to admit it, except to perhaps friends, family, and trusted confidants, leadership is hellishly difficult.

Creativity, Inc.

Ed Catmull & Amy Wallace
LeadershipCraft

Pixar's co-founder explains how the studio protects creative work: candid feedback on early drafts, treating failure as part of the process, and managers whose job is to find and remove the hidden problems that block good people.

If you aren’t experiencing failure, then you are making a far worse mistake: You are being driven by the desire to avoid it.
Management’s job is not to prevent risk but to build the ability to recover.

High Growth Handbook

Elad Gil
Business

A guide to scaling a startup after it finds product-market fit, covering the CEO's role, hiring and managing executives, boards, culture, and company functions, with interviews from founders and operators who have done it.

A CEO’s energy levels dictate those of the team.
“You do not want to preserve culture; you want to collectively steer the right evolution of the culture.” —Patrick Collison

High Output Management

Andrew S. Grove
LeadershipBusiness

Intel's former CEO treats management like production: a manager's output is the output of their team, so their time should go to the work with the most leverage.

The output of a manager is the output of the organizational unit under his or her supervision or influence.
Training is quite simply one of the highest leverage activities a manager can perform.

The Score Takes Care of Itself

Bill Walsh
Leadership

The former 49ers coach explains how he turned a losing team into a champion by setting a standard of performance for every job in the organization, on the view that if the standards and daily habits are right, the wins follow.

Expertise coupled with strength of will is going to prevail.
No one will ever come back to you later and say "thank you" for expecting too little of them.

Leadership in Turbulent Times

Doris Kearns Goodwin
LeadershipHistory

A historian follows Lincoln, Theodore Roosevelt, Franklin Roosevelt, and Lyndon Johnson from early life through national crisis to show how each developed as a leader and what they did when the country needed them.

A finely developed sense of timing—knowing when to wait and when to act—would remain in Lincoln’s repertoire of leadership skills the rest of his life.
“With public sentiment, nothing can fail,” Abraham Lincoln said, “without it nothing can succeed.”

Wooden on Leadership

John Wooden
Leadership

John Wooden, who won ten NCAA titles at UCLA, explains how he led: focus on effort and preparation over winning, and teach character along with skill.

The score will take care of itself when you take care of the effort that precedes the score.
Reputation is what others perceive you as being, and their opinion may be right or wrong. Character, however, is what you really are, and nobody truly knows that but you. But you are what matters most.

Only the Paranoid Survive

Andrew S. Grove
Business

Intel's former CEO describes strategic inflection points, the moments when a business's fundamentals change, and how leaders can recognize them early and commit to a new direction before the old one fails.

A strategic inflection point is a time in the life of a business when its fundamentals are about to change. That change can mean an opportunity to rise to new heights. But it may just as likely signal the beginning of the end.
Strategic change doesn't just start at the top. It starts with your calendar.

Who

Geoff Smart & Randy Street
LeadershipBusiness

A practical hiring method: define the outcomes a role must deliver in a scorecard, source candidates through referrals, and use structured interviews to find people who can actually hit those outcomes.

“Your success as a manager is simply the result of how good you are at hiring the people around you.”
The first failure point of hiring is not being crystal clear about what you really want the person you hire to accomplish.

The Hard Thing About Hard Things

Ben Horowitz
Business

Ben Horowitz on the parts of running a company that management books skip: layoffs, firing executives, and making decisions when every option is bad.

People always ask me, “What’s the secret to being a successful CEO?” Sadly, there is no secret, but if there is one skill that stands out, it’s the ability to focus and make the best move when there are no good moves.
A healthy company culture encourages people to share bad news. A company that discusses its problems freely and openly can quickly solve them. A company that covers up its problems frustrates everyone involved.

Call Sign Chaos

Jim Mattis & Bing West
Leadership

Jim Mattis on what he learned leading Marines over four decades, from commanding a platoon to running U.S. Central Command.

The first is competence. Be brilliant in the basics. Don’t dabble in your job; you must master it.
Remember: As an officer, you need to win only one battle—for the hearts of your troops. Win their hearts and they will win the fights.

Measure What Matters

John Doerr
Business

John Doerr explains OKRs (objectives and key results), the goal-setting system he learned from Andy Grove at Intel, with examples from Google and other companies.

“Bad companies,” Andy wrote, “are destroyed by crisis. Good companies survive them. Great companies are improved by them.”

Good to Great

Jim Collins
Business

Jim Collins and his team studied companies that went from average results to great ones and stayed there, and what set them apart from similar companies that didn't.

Traction

Gino Wickman
Business

A practical system for running a small or mid-sized company, covering vision, people, data, issues, process, and getting the whole team to execute.

The Inspiring Leader

John H. Zenger & Joseph Folkman
Leadership

Zenger and Folkman use data on thousands of leaders to show which behaviors make a leader inspiring, and how to build them.

Team of Rivals

Doris Kearns Goodwin
LeadershipHistory

A history of how Abraham Lincoln brought his political rivals into his cabinet and led them through the Civil War.
